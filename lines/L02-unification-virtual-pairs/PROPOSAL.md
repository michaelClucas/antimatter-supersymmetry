# L02-unification-virtual-pairs: PROPOSAL

**Target SUSY result:** #2, gauge coupling unification (MSSM: alpha_1, alpha_2, alpha_3 meet near 2e16 GeV).

**Question.** Running of gauge couplings is, in hole-theory language, screening and antiscreening by virtual particle-antiparticle pairs. SUSY gets unification by adding superpartner pairs to the vacuum. Can SM antimatter, counted in any SM-equivalent way (Dirac sea, Feynman-Stueckelberg, NCG / division-algebra reformulations restricted to the SM sector), change the running enough to unify?

**Claim (result of this line).** No. [established, reproduced here]
1. In every formulation equivalent to the SM, the beta-function coefficients are functions of the gauge group, the representations and their multiplicities only. A Dirac (or Weyl) field already contains its antiparticle; the loop that "is" the antiparticle is the negative-frequency pole of the same propagator and is counted once. Any other weighting either violates unitarity (the loop is tied to the pair-production rate by the optical theorem) or changes measured quantities (Z partial widths, the R-ratio, Delta alpha_lep, alpha_s running; uniform double counting changes them by a factor ~2), so it is not an equivalent formulation. For W- and Higgs-loop weights the measured-running test is not sensitive enough and the exclusion rests on equivalence plus kappa_V / VH data (WORK 1.6).
2. Structural point (corrected after referee 1): at one loop a complete SU(5) multiplet (5-bar or 10) shifts b_1, b_2, b_3 by the same amount, so any weighting of antiparticle loops that is uniform within each 5-bar and each 10 contributes exactly zero to the one-loop non-unification. Non-uniform weightings do not drop out (antiquark states only: Delta b = (11/30, 1/2, 2/3) per generation). WORK 1.6 / `calc/reweighting.py` classify every single-sector reweighting that could unify (antiquarks x = -5.5, antileptons x = 7.5, Q-loops 1.46, gluon loops 1.126, W loops 0.89, Higgs loops 7.45, ...) and give the kill for each: unitarity (negative weights), LEP Z-pole widths and N_nu (fermion species), alpha_s running (gluon), loss of asymptotic freedom and no physical meeting point (uniform gauge weight 0.134), kappa_V / VH rates plus unitarity (Higgs). For W- and Higgs-loop weights no measured running excludes them; the kill there is equivalence. [derived here]
3. Quantified non-unification (3-loop SM, PDG-2024 inputs): M12 = 10^13.05, M13 = 10^14.25, M23 = 10^16.47 GeV (inputs from tools/sm_inputs.py, alpha_em^-1 = 127.930); minimal spread of alpha^-1 = 3.18 (7.7%) at 10^14.24 GeV; alpha_3^-1 misses the alpha_1 = alpha_2 point by 4.9; exact unification would need alpha_s(MZ) = 0.0739 (measured 0.1180 +- 0.0009). MSSM reference (2-loop, M_SUSY = 2 TeV): spread 0.02, alpha_s predicted 0.118.
4. The spectral action's boundary condition g3 = g2 = sqrt(5/3) g1 at the cutoff is an extra high-scale relation (CONSTRAINTS A3.2) and is not satisfied by SM running anyway; its m_H ~ 170 GeV prediction (hep-th/0610241) was excluded in 2008 and the 2012 repair adds the sigma scalar (forbidden).
5. Proton decay: there is no X boson in the SM, so no physical scale emerges. If one were placed at the SM crossings, M12 and M13 give tau_p ~ 1e24 and 5e28 yr, excluded by Super-K; only M23 (~3e16 GeV) would be safe.
